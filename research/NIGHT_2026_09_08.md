# The night of 2026-09-08: one cell, one result, five instruments that were lying

## What the cell produced

`M_d1`, arm M, task set `orchestrate`, seed 1, GLM-5.3-Flash. Started 11:43:22Z.

**The cascade fired.** At turn 42 the `settle_t1` directive landed and the agent
called `supersede` fifteen times in one turn, retiring 16 of 44 decisions - 36%
of the durable region invalidated by a single directive. Every previous test of
supersession moved one row at a time, where the operation is net-zero by
construction and the predicted "the projected block should shrink" could not
appear. It can now. This is the first data the rerun was actually built to
produce.

**`add_ireland` produced one supersede, not fifteen**, despite text that says
"anything scoped to a single market needs revisiting". If that survives
inspection of the finished run it is the uncascaded-invalidation case - the
agent retires what is named and leaves the dependents standing. Not called yet:
the same hypothesis about an earlier cell was refuted by the data, and the way
to settle it is `./go.sh supersession` on the finished run.

**The cell ended short, as projected.** It exhausted `MAX_MS` at **turn 169 of
240**, wall clock 300.2 of 300 minutes, having delivered **4 of 6 directives**
(turns 42, 82, 123, 162) and never reaching `settle_reversal` at turn 200.
28 supersede calls, 26 applied, 1 provider stall survived, 0 errors.
`study_orchestrate.sh` then halted the study rather than starting `AM_d1`:
"M_d1 never received settle_reversal - the cell cannot test the thing." Correct
refusal; there is no partner cell and `orchestrate` still has exactly one run.

**And it banked itself as `status='done'` with `outcome_tags: []`** - the exact
pooling failure predicted an hour earlier. The tag could not be applied because
the runner had loaded `run.ts` four hours before the fix landed. The read-side
derivation caught it anyway, which is the entire argument for deriving rather
than tagging:

    SCHEDULE_INCOMPLETE  M_d1: 4 of 6 directives delivered
                         (status=done - would otherwise pool as a replicate)

**The supersession result is negative and definitive** (caveat 14). Across 40
boundaries and 26 applied supersessions, the projected block shrank at **zero**
boundaries; `live_items` went 0 -> 127 monotonically. Superseding replaces one
row with another, so it cannot compact - a fifteen-item cascade shows the same
thing a one-item reversal did. The mechanism keeps the projection correct and
does nothing for its size.

## Why it ends short, with the numbers

| | |
|---|---|
| elapsed at turn 141 | 238.2 min of 300 |
| working rate, turns 122-138 | 103 s/turn |
| four turns over ten minutes | t82=20min, t102=17min, t123=25min, t139=31min |

The expensive turns are **getting longer** - 20, 17, 25, 31 - and they are not
outages. They are the cost of a re-projected context grown to ~40k input tokens
emitting 20k+ output tokens per turn. `STUDY_CAVEATS.md` documents arm M's
durable region growing at 209.6 tokens/turn; that growth is now the dominant
cost and it compounds. **The study's own subject arrived as an operational
failure.**

`MAX_MS` was set at 300 min from a 52-turn sample. This run is the real
measurement: 238 min for 141 turns including one 52-minute provider outage. The
schedule needs roughly **420-450 min**, or the directives need to fire earlier.
That is a pooling key, so it is a human decision, not one to make mid-run.

## Five instruments that passed while measuring nothing

Each of these reported confidently and was structurally incapable of seeing what
it claimed to check. None was caught by the test suite; four were caught by an
adversarial audit of the live cell and one by reading a scorer while waiting.

1. **`armsig.test.ts` did not cover the arms the study runs.** It pinned
   A, AP, B, BR, D, O. `study_orchestrate.sh` runs `ARMS="M AM"`. On the day
   `supersede` was added to `AUTONOMOUS_TOOLS` - M and AM's surface and no other
   arm's - a green run was cited as evidence the change was inert. It could not
   have been. Now covers ten arms; M and AM render 7778 and 7187 chars against
   5174-6352 for everything that was pinned.
2. **It also kept a private copy of the accumulating set**, `['A','AP','D']`,
   while `db.ts` says A, AM, AP, AS, D. Adding AM naively would have rendered it
   through a path it never uses and pinned the wrong bytes.
3. **The reversal was scored by a regex that never read the seed's value.**
   `fresh: /T\+?[0-9]/i` is a superset of its own `stale`, so "T+9" graded as
   propagated when the answer was T+6. The non-inferrability the whole design
   rests on was not being checked.
4. **Delivery was detected by pattern over the directive text** - and the
   reversal's text deliberately names no value, so it scored `not_delivered`
   even when delivered.
5. **A reversal cannot be graded by mention, because the answer was already
   true.** It scored `noticed` at turn 77, 123 turns before the directive was
   due.

Plus one that had not fired yet: a cell exhausting `MAX_MS` records
`status='done'`, and the pooling rule is "filter on `status='done'`". M_d1 would
have banked itself as a complete replicate. Now derived from the directives
table, so it works for runs already in flight.

## The one result that changed a published number

`+144.0 (p=0.0015)` on site/GLM-4.7 is **89% claims that named no file**, which
`extract_claims` scores `grounded` by default because nothing was asserted that
could be wrong. Attributed-only: **+16.0, p=0.0093**.

Run on all three cells, exact permutation over 48,620 splits:

| cell | as published | attributed only | free pass |
|---|---|---|---|
| site / 4.7 | +144.0, p=0.0016 | +16.0, p=0.0093 | 89% |
| site / 5.3 | +50.6, p=0.0057 | +37.9, p=0.0103 | 25% |
| operate / 5.3 | +53.1, p=0.0002 | +46.6, p=0.0002 | 12% |

**The finding survives everywhere; only site/4.7's magnitude was inflated** - and
that is the cell where B writes 4.8x more files, which is what a workspace-first
allowlist predicts. Separately the README's `operate` row is stale, in the
direction that strengthens it.

  ceiling effect. This is the finding most worth attacking.
